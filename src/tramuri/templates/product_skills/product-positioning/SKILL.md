# Product Positioning

## When to use
Define or refine how a product should be understood relative to relevant
alternatives.

## When not to use
Do not claim market differentiation before comparing the product to the status
quo or relevant alternatives.

## Inputs
Product value, target audience, buying motivations, competitive research and
source-linked evidence.

## Process
State who the product serves, the outcome it offers, the alternative it replaces
and why the claim is credible. Separate evidence-backed differentiation from
positioning hypotheses.

## Output contract
Return a positioning statement, relevant alternatives, evidence or hypothesis
state, differentiation opportunities and validation gaps.

## Quality checklist
- [ ] Audience and outcome are specific.
- [ ] Alternatives are supported by relevant research.
- [ ] Differentiation claims have sources or are labeled hypotheses.
- [ ] No unsupported market leadership or uniqueness claim is made.
